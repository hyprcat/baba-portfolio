# Case study #1 — Allianz Trade, 40-country rollout

**Section title on site:** "40 countries, one launch playbook"
**Subtitle:** Allianz Trade, Paris · Sept–Dec 2025 · Junior Project Manager

This is the lead case study. The most recent, the most internationally recognisable, and the one whose mechanics map cleanly onto a multi-market launch in food, beauty, or luxury.

---

## Hero metric (big, centered, top of the case study)

> **40 countries · zero deviation flagged late · C-level synthesis weekly**

## Situation

Allianz Trade — the world's largest credit insurer, part of the Allianz Group — runs annual activation programmes across its commercial network. In Q4 2025, the programme covered **40 countries spanning Europe and MESA** (Middle East, South Asia, Africa), with country-level teams running deployment on different cultural calendars, languages, and reporting maturities.

I joined the central programme team in September 2025 as Junior Project Manager.

## Task

Three deliverables, every week:
1. **A consolidated dashboard** showing deployment milestones, deviations, and country-level health across all 40 markets.
2. **An executive synthesis deck** distilling the dashboard into 5 slides for C-level (CMO, COO, regional heads).
3. **Cross-functional liaison** with HR partners across the network — managing communication in English as the working language across teams whose first language was French, Italian, German, Spanish, Arabic, and Hindi.

## Action

- Built **advanced Excel dashboards** using PivotTables, dynamic arrays, and KPI-tracking formulae to consolidate multi-country performance data into one source of truth.
- Designed a **deviation-flagging rule** that surfaced milestone slippage at country level a full week before the central programme sponsors would have noticed otherwise.
- Standardised the **executive deck template** to "one decision per slide" — five slides, five recommended decisions, no narrative filler.
- Held weekly 30-minute syncs with each regional cluster (5 clusters × 30 min = 2.5 hours of structured stakeholder time per week, the rest async).

## Result

- **Zero milestone deviations escalated late** to senior leadership in the four months I owned the dashboard. Every deviation was visible in the dashboard before it became a problem in the field.
- **Executive deck adoption** — the "one decision per slide" template was reused for the Q1 2026 cycle after I left.
- **Cross-cultural fluency** — successfully managed weekly syncs and reporting cadence with country leads in 40 markets without a single language-related miscommunication that affected the programme.

## Reflection — what I learned

> *"In a 40-country rollout, the visual synthesis of the dashboard matters more than its exhaustive completeness. The COMEX doesn't need to read 40 rows; they need to see which 3 markets need attention this week."*

Two specific lessons:

1. **The reporting visual is the product, not the data behind it.** I started the role producing exhaustive reports; I ended it producing 5-slide decks that the COMEX actually read in 3 minutes.
2. **Multi-market activation is fundamentally a coordination problem, not an analytical one.** The math is easy; the *getting the right person to read the dashboard before the milestone slips* is the hard part. Weekly rituals beat post-mortem analysis every time.

## Why this transfers to your team

A multi-country food launch (Elle & Vire across Europe; Pernod Ricard across travel-retail; LVMH Wines & Spirits across W&S Maisons) follows the exact same operating mechanics: country-level activation calendars, central programme coordination, weekly exec synthesis, deviation flagging. I've already done it once at Allianz scale; doing it again with consumer brands instead of insurance products is a translation, not a learning curve.
