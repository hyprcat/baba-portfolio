# Case study #2 — Srikanth Engineering Works, the turnaround

**Section title on site:** "47%, 35%, 28%, 30% — the turnaround"
**Subtitle:** Srikanth Engineering Works, Hyderabad · June 2019 – Sept 2023 · Project Manager

This is the most quantified case study. Four years, four big numbers, one clear arc. It's the one to lead with whenever the role is **insights, marketing analyst, business analyst, or any data-heavy track**.

---

## Hero metric (big, centered)

> **47% bottleneck cut · 35% efficiency gain · 28% cycle compression · 30% process improvement**

(Yes, it's four numbers — the visual idea is to show them as a 2×2 grid, each in a quadrant.)

## Situation

Srikanth Engineering Works is a 300-person agri-tech firm in Hyderabad operating across **5 business units** (manufacturing, finance, ops, supply, distribution). When I joined in June 2019, the senior leadership team was reviewing 5 separate weekly reports — one per BU — that were inconsistent in format, missing comparison baselines, and arriving on different days.

The pattern recurred: by the time leadership saw a problem, the problem had already cost the business something material.

## Task

Two mandates from the senior team:
1. **Build a single source of truth** for cross-BU performance, reviewed weekly by three senior managers, with zero room for "I didn't know."
2. **Find and fix bottlenecks** in delivery cycles and operational efficiency — quantitatively, not based on intuition.

## Action

I split the work into three phases over the 4 years:

### Phase 1 — Build the dashboard (months 1–3)

- Designed a **12-KPI Power BI dashboard** covering output velocity, capacity utilisation, and performance gaps across all 5 BUs.
- Established a **weekly review cadence** with the three senior managers — same time every Monday, 45 minutes, fixed agenda.
- Killed the 5 separate reports. Replaced with one dashboard and a 3-slide synthesis deck.

### Phase 2 — Find the bottlenecks (months 4–7)

- Conducted **end-to-end funnel analysis** across the 300-person organisation — from order intake through to delivery — translating raw operational data into prioritised findings.
- Identified the operational inefficiencies that mattered (the 20% causing 80% of delays) and ignored the noise.

### Phase 3 — Ship the fixes (months 7–24)

- Delivered **prioritised action plans** owned by named individuals with weekly check-ins via JIRA.
- Coordinated **cross-functional plans** between finance and operations — the two BUs whose handoff was the single biggest source of cycle delay.
- Maintained the cadence over 18 months with **zero missed deadlines**.

## Result

- **47% reduction in bottlenecks** within 3 months of the action plans going live.
- **35% increase in operational efficiency** measured at the cross-BU coordination layer.
- **28% compression in delivery cycles** between the cross-functional finance ↔ ops handoff.
- **30% improvement in process outcomes** at the funnel level (order-to-delivery).
- **Zero missed weekly deadlines** for senior management reporting over 18 months.

## Reflection — what I learned

> *"The KPI that matters is the one that triggers an action. In iteration two of the dashboard I removed 8 vanity KPIs — and the dashboard got better, not worse."*

Two specific lessons:

1. **Vanity metrics dilute decision quality.** The first version of the dashboard had 20 KPIs. By month 4, I had cut it to 12. Each cut made the dashboard sharper and the weekly review tighter.
2. **Cross-functional bottlenecks live at the handoff, not inside the function.** Most of the delay wasn't inside finance or inside ops — it was in the JIRA queue between them. Fixing the handoff (clear ownership, weekly check-ins, shared definition-of-done) was 10× more leverage than fixing either function in isolation.

## Why this transfers

This is the case study to lean on whenever the role is in **brand insights, marketing analytics, consumer studies, category management, business analyst, or any role where the toolkit is dashboards + funnel + senior reporting**. Power BI on 5 BUs is the same shape of problem as Power BI on 5 brands, 5 markets, or 5 product categories.
